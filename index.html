⚠ Chemical & Surface DO NOT List

Read before starting any job

Protect clients, cleaning professionals, pets, and property by preventing unsafe chemical combinations and damage to specialty surfaces. When unsure about a product or surface, STOP and verify before proceeding.

Never Mix Cleaning Chemicals

  • Never mix bleach with ammonia.
  • Never mix bleach with vinegar or other acids.
  • Never mix bleach with toilet bowl cleaners or rust removers.
  • Do not combine cleaning products unless the manufacturer explicitly says the combination is safe.
  • One:18 rule: one cleaning product at a time. Remove/rinse a prior product as directed before changing products.

Products Not to Use Unless Specifically Appropriate / Approved

  • Chlorine bleach / bleach-based cleaners — not for routine use on fabrics, delicate finishes, natural stone, metals, or unknown surfaces.
  • Abrasive powders, steel wool, pumice stones, harsh scouring pads — may scratch stainless steel, glass, acrylic, fiberglass, cooktops, polished fixtures, and stone.
  • Magic Erasers / melamine foam — can dull or remove finish on painted walls, glossy cabinetry, finished wood, stainless steel, high-gloss surfaces.
  • Vinegar / acidic cleaners — never on marble, travertine, limestone, or other acid-sensitive natural stone.
  • Harsh cleaners on quartz — avoid highly acidic/alkaline cleaners, oven cleaner, abrasive powders, and aggressive degreasers unless manufacturer-approved.
  • Oven cleaner — do not use on counters, appliance exteriors, aluminum, cabinetry, floors, or general kitchen surfaces.
  • Toilet bowl cleaner — keep inside the toilet bowl; not a shower, grout, faucet, sink, countertop, tub, or general bathroom cleaner.
  • Furniture polish / oily products — never apply to flooring due to residue and slip risk.
  • Excess dish soap on floors — may leave sticky film, streaks, and dirt-attracting residue.

Client-Provided & Unlabeled Products

  • A client request does not automatically make a product safe for a surface. Pause and verify when damage is possible.
  • Never use products in unlabeled bottles, unknown homemade mixtures, or chemicals whose contents cannot be identified.
  • Follow product labels, manufacturer surface-care instructions, and Safety Data Sheets (SDS) where applicable.

Never "Experiment" on a Client's Property

  • Do not keep escalating to stronger chemicals simply because a stain remains.
  • Remaining discoloration may be permanent staining, etching, finish damage, mineral deposits, bleaching, dye transfer, or deterioration.
  • A stain that remains is better than a permanently damaged surface. Document the condition and report it.

The One:18 5-Second Check

  1. What surface is this?
  2. Is this product approved for that surface?
  3. Have I used another chemical here?
  4. Could this scratch, discolor, etch, or strip the finish?
  5. If I am unsure, have I stopped and verified?
WHEN IN DOUBT — DO NOT USE IT.
